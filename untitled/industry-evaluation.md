# Industry Evaluation

### The global market

According to the OECD's Global Plastics Outlook, annual plastic production increased from 234 million tonnes in 2000 to 460 million tonnes in 2019.

During the same period, annual plastic waste increased from 156 million tonnes to 353 million tonnes.

Only 9% of plastic waste was ultimately recycled. [OECD](https://www.oecd.org/en/publications/global-plastics-outlook_de747aef-en/full-report/component-6.html)

This creates a structural gap: plastic is produced at industrial scale, but recovery remains comparatively weak.

The opportunity is therefore not simply to create more recycling machines. It is to improve the flow of material into those machines.

### Nigeria

Nigeria's plastic industry has a large network of manufacturers, importers, packaging companies, retailers, collectors and recyclers.

UNIDO's study of the Nigerian value chain identifies plastic raw-material producers, polymer manufacturers, compounders, packaging producers, distributors, retailers, plastic-waste collectors and recyclers as participants in the value chain. [UNIDO](https://www.unido.org/sites/default/files/unido-publications/2022-12/Plastic-value-chain-in-Nigeria.pdf)

The country is also seeing investment in recycling infrastructure.

In 2025, IFC announced a US$37 million financing partnership with Mohinani Group covering PET recycling plants in Ghana and Nigeria. Each plant is designed to produce up to 15,000 tonnes of recycled PET resin annually, with approximately 90% of raw material expected from local small businesses involved in plastic collection. [IFC](https://www.ifc.org/en/pressroom/2025/ifc-partners-with-mohinani-group-to-drive-plastic-recycling-and-manufacturing-in-w)

This demonstrates an important point for Bottle Up: large-scale recycling capacity still depends on many smaller collection activities feeding material into the system.

### Collection infrastructure

In March 2026, UNIDO and the Government of Japan handed over a pilot plastic waste collection centre in Abuja. The facility is expected to collect up to 1,200 tonnes of plastic waste annually and is designed to improve collection, sorting and recycling links. [UNIDO](https://www.unido.org/news/unido-and-government-japan-handover-pilot-plastic-waste-collection-centre-abuja-nigeria)

### Regulation

Nigeria's environmental framework around plastics is becoming more structured. NESREA lists the National Environmental (Plastic Waste Control) Regulations, 2026, and provides guidance for Extended Producer Responsibility (EPR) in packaging. [NESREA](https://nesrea.gov.ng/laws-regulations/)

This creates a growing need for reliable information about what is collected, where it was collected, how much was recovered, what material it was, who handled it and where it went.
