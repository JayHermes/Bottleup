## Founder & Company Statement

Bottle Up started with a simple observation: plastic waste already has value, but that value is often lost because there is no easy way for ordinary people to participate in the collection process.

A bottle thrown into a drain is waste. The same bottle collected, verified, sorted and delivered to a recycler becomes a raw material.

Bottle Up is being built around that difference.

Our goal is to make it easier for people to return plastic waste, easier for collectors and recyclers to access usable materials, and easier for businesses to track and support the recovery of the plastic they put into the market.

We are starting in Nigeria, beginning with communities where we can directly test the model, improve collection operations and build relationships with recycling partners.

Bottle Up is not trying to solve every part of waste management at once.

We are starting with one clear problem:

> **How do we get more plastic from where it becomes waste to where it can be used again?**

## Executive Summary

Plastic recycling depends on one thing before anything else: getting usable plastic waste into the recycling value chain.

The global plastics industry produces hundreds of millions of tonnes of plastic every year. In 2019, global plastic production reached approximately 460 million tonnes, while plastic waste reached 353 million tonnes. Only 9% of plastic waste was ultimately recycled. [OECD](https://www.oecd.org/en/publications/global-plastics-outlook_de747aef-en/full-report/component-6.html)

Nigeria faces the same structural problem at a different scale.

Plastic is widely used across food packaging, bottled and sachet water, household products, retail and manufacturing. Yet collection, sorting, transportation and reliable access to recycling markets remain fragmented.

Bottle Up sits between the point where plastic becomes waste and the businesses that can recover its value.

The model is simple:

**People collect plastic → Bottle Up coordinates pickup and verification → material moves to recycling partners → users receive rewards → businesses can support and track recovery.**

Bottle Up launched its first pilot on **28 September 2026** in **Ikot Ekpene, Uyo and Eket**.

The first 24-hour snapshot recorded:

* **8 users signed up**
* **7 pickup requests**
* **3 launch locations**

These figures are early traction, not a final pilot result. The pilot is intended to test demand, pickup logistics, material verification, reward mechanics and recycler relationships before expansion.

## The Plastic Problem

Plastic itself is not the problem.

Plastic is useful because it is lightweight, durable, cheap to produce and suitable for many applications.

The problem begins when a product reaches the end of its useful life and there is no efficient route for it to return to the production cycle.

A plastic bottle can therefore follow two very different paths.

**Path A — Lost value**

Consumer → discarded → drain/street/dumpsite → environmental leakage

**Path B — Recovered value**

Consumer → collection → sorting → recycling → recycled material → new product

The difference between these paths is largely a collection problem.

The OECD estimates that nearly two-thirds of global plastic waste comes from applications with lifespans of less than five years, with packaging alone accounting for 40%. [OECD](https://www.oecd.org/en/publications/global-plastics-outlook_de747aef-en/full-report/component-6.html)

## Industry Evaluation

### The global market

According to the OECD's Global Plastics Outlook, annual plastic production increased from 234 million tonnes in 2000 to 460 million tonnes in 2019.

During the same period, annual plastic waste increased from 156 million tonnes to 353 million tonnes.

Only 9% of plastic waste was ultimately recycled. [OECD](https://www.oecd.org/en/publications/global-plastics-outlook_de747aef-en/full-report/component-6.html)

This creates a structural gap: plastic is produced at industrial scale, but recovery remains comparatively weak.

The opportunity is therefore not simply to create more recycling machines. It is to improve the flow of material into those machines.

### Nigeria

Nigeria's plastic industry has a large network of manufacturers, importers, packaging companies, retailers, collectors and recyclers.

UNIDO's study of the Nigerian value chain identifies plastic raw-material producers, polymer manufacturers, compounders, packaging producers, distributors, retailers, plastic-waste collectors and recyclers as participants in the value chain. [UNIDO](https://www.unido.org/sites/default/files/unido-publications/2022-12/Plastic-value-chain-in-Nigeria.pdf)

The country is also seeing investment in recycling infrastructure.

In 2025, IFC announced a US$37 million financing partnership with Mohinani Group covering PET recycling plants in Ghana and Nigeria. Each plant is designed to produce up to 15,000 tonnes of recycled PET resin annually, with approximately 90% of raw material expected from local small businesses involved in plastic collection. [IFC](https://www.ifc.org/en/pressroom/2025/ifc-partners-with-mohinani-group-to-drive-plastic-recycling-and-manufacturing-in-w)

This demonstrates an important point for Bottle Up: large-scale recycling capacity still depends on many smaller collection activities feeding material into the system.

### Collection infrastructure

In March 2026, UNIDO and the Government of Japan handed over a pilot plastic waste collection centre in Abuja. The facility is expected to collect up to 1,200 tonnes of plastic waste annually and is designed to improve collection, sorting and recycling links. [UNIDO](https://www.unido.org/news/unido-and-government-japan-handover-pilot-plastic-waste-collection-centre-abuja-nigeria)

### Regulation

Nigeria's environmental framework around plastics is becoming more structured. NESREA lists the National Environmental (Plastic Waste Control) Regulations, 2026, and provides guidance for Extended Producer Responsibility (EPR) in packaging. [NESREA](https://nesrea.gov.ng/laws-regulations/)

This creates a growing need for reliable information about what is collected, where it was collected, how much was recovered, what material it was, who handled it and where it went.

## Nigeria's Recycling Value Chain

A simplified Nigerian plastic recycling chain is:

**Plastic producers → manufacturers/packaging companies → consumers → collection → sorting & processing → recyclers/manufacturers → new products**

Around this chain are government agencies, producer responsibility organisations, informal collectors, businesses, community groups and other intermediaries.

UNIDO's Nigerian value-chain research similarly identifies producers, packaging companies, distributors, retailers, collectors and recyclers as interconnected participants. [UNIDO](https://www.unido.org/sites/default/files/unido-publications/2022-12/Plastic-value-chain-in-Nigeria.pdf)

#### Bottle Up's position

Bottle Up does not need to own every part of this chain.

Its role is to strengthen the connection between:

**plastic waste → collection → verification → recycling market**

## The Gap Bottle Up Is Addressing

#### For individuals

People may have recyclable plastic but do not know where to take it, do not have time to transport it, do not know whether it has value, or receive little immediate benefit from separating it.

#### For collectors

Collectors need reliable sources of material, predictable pickup routes, better access to households and businesses, and information about where material is available.

#### For recyclers

Recyclers need consistent material supply, appropriate material types, acceptable quality and predictable volumes.

#### For businesses

Companies increasingly need to understand what happens to packaging and plastic materials connected to their operations. Collection data, however, can be fragmented.

Bottle Up is designed around this gap.

## What Bottle Up Is

Bottle Up is a plastic collection and recovery platform.

The core product allows people to:

1. collect recyclable material
2. request pickup
3. have the material verified
4. receive points based on verified material
5. redeem available rewards

At the other end of the system, Bottle Up works with recycling partners and businesses to move collected material into the recycling value chain.

The platform therefore serves two sides of the market.

**Supply side:** people, households, collectors and organisations with recyclable material.

**Demand side:** recyclers, manufacturers and businesses that need recovered material or recovery services.

## How Bottle Up Works

**User → Collection → Bottle Up pickup → Verification → Rewards → Recycling partner → Recycled material → New products**

The system is deliberately simple.

> **Collect. Request pickup. Get verified. Get rewarded.**

***

## User Journey

#### 1. Collect

A user collects recyclable plastic from their home, workplace, school, business or community.

#### 2. Request

The user opens Bottle Up and requests a pickup.

#### 3. Pickup

Bottle Up or an assigned collection partner picks up the material. Pickup is intended to be part of the normal Bottle Up service.

#### 4. Verify

The material is checked for weight, material type, contamination and general quality.

#### 5. Points

Verified material earns points according to Bottle Up's reward rules.

#### 6. Redeem

Users can redeem eligible rewards through available options.

#### 7. Recovery

The material is aggregated and transferred to an appropriate recycling partner.

***

## Collection & Verification

Collection is one of the most important parts of the Bottle Up model.

Simply collecting large quantities of plastic is not enough. The material must be useful to the next participant in the chain.

Bottle Up intends to record:

* user
* collection location
* material category
* weight
* collection date
* verification status
* destination
* recycler/partner
* reward issued

Over time, this creates a record of material movement rather than simply a record of users.

#### Initial material focus

Bottle Up can begin with commonly traded recyclable materials such as:

* PET bottles
* PP
* PE
* HDPE
* LDPE
* other materials accepted by confirmed recycling partners

The exact accepted-material list will depend on partner requirements and local economics.

***

## Reward System

Bottle Up uses rewards to give people a practical reason to participate consistently.

The current base concept is:

**100 Bottle Up points = 1 kg of verified recyclable material.**

The actual monetary or non-monetary value of points should be determined according to Bottle Up's operating economics and available reward partners rather than promising an unsustainable fixed cash value.

Rewards may eventually include:

* airtime
* data
* cash-equivalent benefits
* partner discounts
* other useful services

The objective is not to make recycling feel like a game. It is to give people a reason to keep participating.

## Business & Revenue Model

Bottle Up has two sides.

**Consumer side:** users supply recyclable materials.

**Business side:** recyclers and businesses create revenue opportunities.

#### Revenue flow

**Material aggregation → recycling partner → material sales**

**Business recovery program → collection/reporting service → business fee**

**Verified recovery data → reporting → business service**

### Revenue stream 1 — Material aggregation

Bottle Up can earn through the sale of aggregated recyclable material to recycling partners.

The margin depends on:

**sale price − collection cost − logistics − sorting/handling − rewards − operating costs**

### Revenue stream 2 — Business recovery programs

Businesses that produce or use significant quantities of plastic can work with Bottle Up to support collection and recovery.

Potential services include collection campaigns, employee/customer recycling programs, community recovery programs and recovery reporting.

### Revenue stream 3 — Recovery data and reporting

As collection volume increases, Bottle Up can provide businesses with verified records of kilograms recovered, material types, collection locations, dates and recycling partner destination.

## Unit Economics Framework

Bottle Up should measure its economics per kilogram rather than only counting users.

#### Revenue per kg

**Material sale revenue/kg + business recovery revenue/kg**

#### Variable cost per kg

**Collection + transport + sorting + verification + rewards + payment costs**

#### Contribution margin

**Revenue/kg − variable cost/kg**

For each pickup:

**Pickup contribution = material revenue + service revenue − pickup cost − rewards − handling cost**

The key question is:

> **Does collecting one more kilogram of plastic create or destroy value?**

The numbers should be measured during the 2026 pilot rather than invented in advance.

## Risks & Mitigation

#### Low user retention

**Risk:** people sign up once and stop collecting.

**Mitigation:** improve rewards, pickup reliability and user experience while measuring repeat behaviour.

#### High logistics costs

**Risk:** a pickup containing very little material can become uneconomical.

**Mitigation:** increase collection density, batch pickups and optimise routes.

#### Poor-quality material

**Risk:** contaminated or mixed materials may have lower value.

**Mitigation:** clear collection guidelines, verification and partner-specific material standards.

#### Unstable recycling prices

**Risk:** plastic prices can change.

**Mitigation:** maintain relationships with multiple buyers and avoid depending on one material or buyer.

#### Fraud

**Risk:** users may attempt to submit invalid material, duplicate collections or manipulate weight.

**Mitigation:** physical verification, collection records, anomaly detection and controlled redemption.

#### Partner dependency

**Risk:** Bottle Up may initially depend heavily on a small number of recycling companies.

**Mitigation:** develop multiple downstream relationships before scaling collection aggressively.

#### Reward economics

**Risk:** rewards could cost more than the value generated by collected material.

**Mitigation:** monitor contribution margin per kg and adjust reward structures based on actual economics.

#### Regulation

**Risk:** waste-management and plastics regulation can vary by state and evolve.

**Mitigation:** maintain appropriate registrations and partnerships and monitor applicable environmental requirements.

***

## What Bottle Up Will Not Do

Bottle Up will not attempt to become:

* a general waste-management company overnight
* a plastic manufacturer
* a recycling plant in every city
* a replacement for existing recyclers
* a platform that accepts every type of waste immediately

The initial focus is narrower:

> **Collect recyclable plastic, verify it, reward participation and move the material to credible recycling channels.**

That focus allows the business to learn the economics before expanding its scope.
